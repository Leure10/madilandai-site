A journal that does not talk down to her

Most journals written for teenage girls are either far too young for them or read like a worksheet. This one was built on a simple idea: ask her real questions and then get out of the way. Ninety-six prompts, each one different.

The prompts move from easy to honest. Early pages ask about songs, Tuesdays and things she is good at. Later ones ask what the voice in her head actually said today, and whether she would say that sentence out loud to a friend. The answers in “See an answered page” are made up.

Why undated matters

Dated journals create a deadline. One missed week and the whole thing feels like a failure, so it goes in a drawer. These prompts can be answered in any order, at any pace. She opens it on a page that matches her day.

This journal is for reflection and self-expression. It is not a substitute for support from a qualified professional.
