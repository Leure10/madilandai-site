A story on the left, its picture on the right

Jodie is staying at Bodie's house tonight, and she has big plans: she is going to stay up ALL night. Then the house goes quiet, the moon looks like a swing, and Bodie picks up his crayons. He draws a small round cloud, and it floats right off the paper!

Up they go on Tuft, past Maple Street, a sleepy cat, a pond of singing frogs, and a plump little owl who is just getting up. On the moon they find Speck, the smallest star in the sky, curled up on a cloud and much too tired to shine. So the girl who is never sleepy teaches a little star how to stay awake, and Bodie notices something that nobody else does.

Read together, then color the scene

Younger children listen and color; early readers can read the story on their own. The line art is simple, with big shapes for small hands. A story that winds down: it starts with running, bouncing, and giggling, then the pages turn quieter and the words get softer and fewer, so the last pages are gentle to read aloud at the end of the day.

What it gently shows

  • Noisy night-time sounds can be nice sounds
  • A loud friend and a quiet friend can look after each other
  • Helping someone small do a big thing
  • Imagination, drawing, and noticing little things
